Your puppy's first 30 days: a calm, confident start

MKDr. Meera KulkarniConsulting vet · Reviewed by Neha Joshi 28 Aug 2026 8 min read

Bringing home a puppy is pure joy, and a little chaos. The first month shapes how your dog sees the world, so a calm, consistent start pays off for years. Here's the plan our trainers and vets share with every new puppy family.

Before your puppy comes home

Choose a quiet corner for a crate or pen, and puppy-proof anything at nose height: cables, shoes, houseplants and bins. Stock up on the food your breeder or shelter used so tummies don't get upset by a sudden change.

  • A crate or pen big enough to stand, turn and stretch in
  • Two bowls, a lightweight collar with ID tag and a 1.5 m leash
  • Three or four toys of different textures, including one to chew
  • Enzyme cleaner for accidents (they will happen!)

Week one: settling in

Keep the first few days boring on purpose. Limit visitors, follow the same routine for meals, naps and play, and let your puppy explore one room at a time. Puppies sleep 18–20 hours a day. Protect those naps.

“Predictability is the best comfort you can give a puppy. When they know what comes next, they relax, and relaxed puppies learn faster.” — Neha Joshi, head groomer & trainer

Toilet training that actually works

Take your puppy outside after every nap, meal and play session, and every two hours in between. Use the same spot and a cue word, and celebrate the moment they finish with a tiny treat. Never punish accidents. Just clean up with an enzyme spray and supervise more closely.

Trainer tip: A puppy can usually hold its bladder for about one hour per month of age, plus one. A three-month-old needs a break roughly every four hours overnight.

The socialisation window

Between three and fourteen weeks, puppies are wired to accept new things. Aim for gentle, positive exposure to different people, surfaces, sounds and calm, vaccinated dogs. Quality beats quantity: one good experience is worth ten overwhelming ones.

Vet visits & vaccinations

Book a check-up in the first week. Your vet will plan core vaccinations, deworming and flea prevention, and can microchip at the same visit. Ask about our Puppy Intro groom too. A short, treat-filled first visit makes future grooms much easier.

Your 30-day checklist

  1. Crate set up and puppy-proofing done
  2. First vet visit booked and vaccination plan agreed
  3. Toilet routine and cue word in place
  4. Ten new, positive experiences each week
  5. Name, sit and “touch” started in 2-minute sessions
  6. Puppy Intro groom booked around week four
