AQUASCAPE ARCHITECTURE

Planting in three layers.
Depth, flow, and perspective.

Natural underwater landscapes create the illusion of infinite depth by layering low foreground carpets, textured midground hardscape, and towering background stem forests.

0–5 cm height

The Foreground Carpet

Low-profile creeping plants like Monte Carlo and Marsilea hirsuta create rolling green meadows across the substrate, reflecting light and making the aquarium feel instantly larger.

5–20 cm height

The Midground Hardscape

Epiphytes like Anubias Nana Petite, Bucephalandra, and Cryptocoryne attached to driftwood crevices soften rock transitions and provide shady resting nooks for nano fish.

20–45+ cm height

The Background Canopy

Fast-growing vertical stems like Rotala rotundifolia and Vallisneria act as natural biological nutrient sponges, concealing technical gear and offering tall shelter.

GROWING QUESTIONS

Make a little room for learning.

Do all aquarium plants need added CO₂?

No. Some plants can grow without added CO₂ when their light and nutrient needs are met. More demanding layouts need a specific plan.

Should I bury every plant’s roots?

Planting depends on growth form. Rhizome plants such as Anubias and Java fern should not have their rhizomes buried. Rooted stem and rosette plants are planted differently.

How do I choose plants for my fish?

Research whether your fish dig, nibble plants, need open space, or prefer shelter. Choose safe planting and preserve room for natural behavior.
