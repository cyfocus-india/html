PLANTED TANKS

A greener tank starts with the right plants.

Grow a layout that works with your aquarium.

01

Choose for your conditions

Begin with the aquarium’s light, available space, and the maintenance routine you can sustain. Choose plants that fit those conditions instead of trying to change everything at once.

Look at mature plant size and growth form. A small pot can become a much larger presence, and a beautiful foreground depends on allowing room to grow.

02

Give the layout a little structure

Set a focal point with aquarium-safe hardscape, then think about foreground, midground, and background. Leave clear swimming space and access to equipment.

A small number of thoughtfully arranged plants can make a more coherent beginning than many varieties competing for attention.

03

Plant the right part

Different plants need different placement. Rhizome plants, including Anubias and Java fern, should have the rhizome exposed rather than buried under substrate.

Stem plants, mosses, and rosette plants have other planting methods. Identify what you have and follow the supplier’s species-specific planting instructions.

04

Find a repeatable rhythm

Keep a consistent lighting schedule. Choose nutrition appropriate to the planting and follow the product instructions carefully.

Observe how the plants respond before making large changes. New growth, leaf condition, and the way the layout fills in are useful signals to record.

05

Make space as it grows

Remove deteriorating leaves and trim plants thoughtfully to retain the shape and open space you planned. Check that growth does not block filter intakes or crowd the surface.

Use suitable aquarium tools and keep all materials safe for the inhabitants. Plan plant choices around fish that may dig or nibble foliage.

06

Follow the growth form

A clear planting reference is useful when you bring home an unfamiliar species. Compare the supplier’s instructions with the actual plant in your hand.
