Safety Fit-Out

What Makes a Training Car Different

A learner car is not a normal car with a sign on the roof. Four things are added or changed before any vehicle joins the fleet.

Instructor Dual Controls

A second brake and clutch pedal on the passenger side, mechanically linked to the primary pedals. Your instructor can stop the car in the same distance you could.

Third & Fourth Mirrors

An additional interior mirror and a passenger-side wing mirror let the instructor watch your observation habits and the road independently of you.

Reverse Camera & Sensors

Rear sensors and a camera on every four-wheeler, used as a teaching aid during bay parking and then deliberately switched off once you can judge it unaided.

First-Aid & Fire Kit

A sealed first-aid box and a 1kg ABC fire extinguisher are carried in every vehicle, checked monthly against their expiry dates.

Maintenance

Every Vehicle, Every Day

Training cars cover far more clutch cycles and hard stops than a private car, so the inspection routine is correspondingly strict.

  1. Before the first shift

    Pre-Shift Safety Check

    Brakes, dual-control linkage, tyre pressure and tread, lights, indicators, horn, mirrors and fluid levels — signed off by the instructor taking the car out.

  2. Between students

    Cabin Reset

    Steering wheel, gear lever, handbrake and door handles wiped down; seat and mirrors returned to neutral so the next learner sets them up themselves.

  3. End of day

    Fault Log & Fuel

    Any pull, judder, warning light or unusual noise is logged against the vehicle. Anything safety-related takes the car off the roster until it is cleared.

  4. Every 30 days

    Workshop Service

    Full mechanical service including clutch wear measurement, brake pad depth, alignment, and a dual-control function test at our own service bay.

  5. Annually

    Compliance Renewal

    Insurance, pollution certificate and fitness certification renewed, plus expiry checks on the first-aid kit and fire extinguisher.

Decision Guide

Which Vehicle Should You Learn In?

The manual-versus-automatic question decides what your licence lets you drive afterwards, so it is worth a minute's thought before you book.

  • Learn manual if you want a licence valid for both transmissions, you expect to drive older or borrowed cars, or you may drive commercially later.
  • Learn automatic if you drive mostly in heavy city traffic, you have a knee or left-leg limitation, or clutch anxiety is what has stopped you before.
  • Learn on a scooter first if you have never operated a motor vehicle — balance and throttle sense transfer usefully to everything after.
  • Ask for the sedan or MUV if you will be driving a larger family car, since width judgement is a genuinely different skill.
